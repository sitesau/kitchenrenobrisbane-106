Kitchen Renovations Brisbane How to Choose: Pick the right scope first

Last updated: November 2026

Key takeaway

Choosing a Brisbane kitchen project starts with scope, not style. A surface refresh usually suits worn finishes and a fixed footprint; a standard renovation replaces cabinets, benchtops, splashbacks, lighting and appliances; a custom build reworks storage, circulation and pantry access. Published ranges are about $15,000 to $60,000+, with cabinetry often near 25 to 40 percent of the total. Makeovers may run about two to four weeks, standard work four to eight weeks, and custom projects eight to twelve weeks. In Queensland, building work over $3,300 requires current QBCC licensing, and plumbing or electrical components must be done by licensed trades.

Kitchen Renovations Brisbane How to Choose Explained

Before shortlisting renovators, describe the kitchen in plain terms. Is the footprint basically workable, with tired doors, benchtops and splashback? A refresh may be enough. Are cabinets failing, storage chaotic and appliances poorly placed? That points toward replacement within the existing layout. If pantry access, movement between sink, cooktop and fridge, or connection to living areas is the complaint, the brief is closer to custom work.

  • Refresh: keep layout, update visible surfaces, lighting and hardware.
  • Standard: replace cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, appliances and task lighting.
  • Custom: redesign storage, workflow, pantry reach and how the room links with adjoining spaces.

That tier decision controls cost, duration and the mix of licensed trades. It also stops a quote for a simple facelift being compared with a quote for a full redesign.

Build one brief every bidder prices

Different totals are hard to judge unless each renovator prices the same problem. Write a one-page brief with photos, measurements where available, the must-keep elements, the items that must move, and the outcome the room needs to deliver. State whether the footprint is fixed or open to change, and list appliances that are staying.

Ask each bidder to respond to identical inclusions: cabinet type, benchtop material, splashback, lighting, appliance integration, plumbing, electrical work, disposal, protective works and whether 3D drawings are included.   • Request exclusions in writing, not verbally.
  • Ask how allowances are calculated and what happens if selections exceed them.
  • Check that each quote references the same drawings or written scope.

Normalise quotes before comparing totals

A lower figure can simply be a narrower scope. Put quotes side by side and mark each line as included, excluded, allowance or provisional. Pay particular attention to cabinetry, since it is commonly the largest single item and can sit near 25 to 40 percent of the budget. Drawer construction, hardware, internal organisers and corner solutions often explain real differences between two similar-looking proposals.

Benchtops need a direct question because traditional engineered quartz is ruled out in the guidance due to silicosis concerns, leaving silica-free stone, laminate and porcelain as practical paths. Lighting also deserves detail: under-cabinet strips, pendants and task fittings each serve a different purpose, so a single generic line item may hide missing zones.

  • Confirm installation method and care needs for each surface.
  • Ask who supplies appliances and whether integration affects cabinetry dimensions.
  • Compare warranty wording on cabinets, benchtops and installation.

Licence and coordination checks for Queensland work

For building work over $3,300 in Queensland, ask for current QBCC licence details relevant to the quoted job. Plumbing and electrical components should be handled by licensed plumbers and electricians, and the quote should show who books them, when they appear and who signs off each stage.

If one contractor manages the whole project, that responsibility should be explicit. If separate trades are engaged, the written program should sequence cabinetry, plumbing, electrical, plastering, painting and benchtop installation so one trade is not waiting on another without a clear owner. Keep decisions anchored to the property, drawings and written inclusions rather than a verbal promise.

Useful reference points include the Housing Industry Association, Master Builders Australia and ASIC Moneysmart renovating guidance for general planning context, while licensing specifics remain a quote-stage verification task.

Who this decision path suits

This approach fits Brisbane owners planning a makeover, standard renovation or custom kitchen where storage, layout, surfaces or appliances are the real frustration. It also helps owners preparing a home for resale who want to understand what the kitchen contributes before committing to a redesign.

It is less useful if the goal is a universal fixed price without a site visit, because realistic quoting produces ranges rather than one universal figure. It also does not replace a licensed assessment where plumbing, electrical changes, structural questions or compliance concerns are involved.

A practical next step is to photograph the existing room, mark what stays and what must move, choose a working budget band, then request that every renovator price the same written scope.   1. Define the failure. Write down whether the kitchen fails on storage, layout, worn finishes, appliance placement or several issues at once.
  2. Set a working band. Use $15,000 to $60,000+ as a planning frame, then ask what scope realistically fits inside it.
  3. Verify licences. Check current QBCC licensing for building work over $3,300 and licensed plumbers and electricians for those components.
  4. Compare like with like. Give every bidder the same brief, drawings and inclusions list, then compare inclusions, exclusions and allowances line by line.

Common questions

Should I choose a makeover, standard renovation or custom kitchen? Choose by scope. Keep the footprint if the layout works and surfaces are tired. Move to standard replacement when cabinets, storage, benchtops and appliances are the issue. Choose custom when workflow, pantry access or the link with living areas must change.

What budget should I use before getting quotes? Treat $15,000 to $60,000+ as a broad Brisbane planning range, recognising premium custom kitchens can exceed it. Cabinetry is often the largest budget item at roughly 25 to 40 percent, so storage and hardware choices deserve early attention.

Which compliance checks matter before signing? Confirm current QBCC licensing for Queensland building work over $3,300, and require licensed plumbers and electricians for their parts. The quote should also name who coordinates trades and who approves each stage before the next begins.
